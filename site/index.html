Research

The full picture

From the core of a star to a photon in a telescope. I compute how axions are made, model the stars that make them, and analyse the X- and gamma-ray data myself.

i · Hot, dense matter

Stars and supernovae as axion factories

Stripped-envelope supernovae, Betelgeuse, the Sun and the massive stars of the galaxy M82 all make axions. NuSTAR and Fermi-LAT are the detectors.

ii · Cosmic-scale converters

Diffuse backgrounds and cluster magnets

Every supernova in cosmic history adds to a diffuse axion background. Galaxy clusters are megaparsec-sized magnets that can even turn high-frequency gravitational waves into light.

iii · Instruments

X-ray optics for IAXO

The NuSTAR glass-optics inventory, synchrotron measurements and optics simulations for the International Axion Observatory’s X-ray telescopes.

Stripped-envelope supernovae for QCD axion detection

Stars that lose their outer layers before exploding carry the strongest surface magnetic fields of any supernova progenitor, ideal for turning axions into gamma rays.

Published in Physical Review Letters

Small progenitors, large couplings: Type Ic supernova constraints on radiatively decaying particles

Compact progenitors let photons from particles that decay close to the star escape, which probes couplings SN 1987A can’t reach.

Led analysis · Corresponding author · PRL 137, 061004 (2026)

Published in Physical Review Letters

NuSTAR bounds on radiatively decaying particles from M82

Heavy particles from M82’s massive stars would decay into X-rays on the 3.5 Mpc trip to Earth. Decay needs no magnetic field, which removes the biggest uncertainty of conversion searches.

Led analysis · PRL 134, 171004 (2025)
